## What we take, and what it changes

| Reference behaviour | Nerdy equivalent | Change it makes to our plan |
|---|---|---|
| Narrated tour, "skip anytime with Return to Browse" | Dee narrates the rundown; a named button skips it at any moment | The skip must be a **real, always-visible control**, not a Skip card on the first stop |
| Discovery wall with a navigation bar **at the bottom** | Our toolbar under the wall: Featured · Newest · Most viewed | Confirms the owner's layout |
| Point at a thumbnail, press trigger | Point at a tile, press trigger | Rundown stop 1 teaches exactly this |
| **Gear in the corner** opens app settings: volume, show/hide button labels, other | Our gear: volumes, button labels, language, captions, haptics, replay rundown, clear data | "Show or hide button labels" is a real setting there too — ours stays |
| **Environment selector next to the gear**, sets the default browsing scenery | Our AR / VR choice sits **next to the gear in the toolbar**, not buried in settings | Matches the owner's "decide between AR and VR quickly"; the arrival still asks once |
| Tutorial continues **inside real content**, not only in the lobby | Browse controls are taught at the wall; hands-on controls are taught **at a real workbench** | Splits our rundown into two places instead of five stops in the lounge |
| "Helper UI" panel in front of you showing the controls being described | A controller diagram card that appears during the rundown and follows the button being named | New: we had Dee pointing, but no diagram |
| Show/Hide Panel button reveals playback controls | Our assistant bar (Pause, Mute, Music, Again, Help) | Teach the bar the same way: name the button, make them press it |
| Blue **blinking** continue affordance on the bar and the controller | Our Next / continue on the lesson card | Borrow the blink: the continue affordance pulses until pressed |
| Bookmarks into a Saved section | Not in the owner's toolbar (Featured / Newest / Most viewed) | Deliberately dropped; can return later without changing the layout |
| Free fly mode, multiple viewpoints | **Nothing.** No locomotion, no camera angles | Explicit non-goals; the transcript's flying section has no Nerdy analogue |
| "Return to Browse" as a panic button for motion | "Back to the wall" available at all times, including mid-lesson | Ours is about leaving, not motion sickness — we have no locomotion — but the always-available exit is worth copying |

## Ordering lesson

The reference teaches in the order the learner meets things: browse controls at the wall, then content controls
inside content, each one gated on the learner actually pressing the button. Our rundown follows the same shape:
point and pick at the wall; grab, the assistant bar and the table handle at a workbench.
